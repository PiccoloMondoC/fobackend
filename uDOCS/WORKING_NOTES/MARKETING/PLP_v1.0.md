Yes. I think we landed somewhere important. Here's the consolidated summary.

## Sagrenti — Public Language & Positioning Summary

### 1. We don't need to abandon the architecture

**Future Offering** remains a strong **internal/domain term**.

Engineering needs a single concept capable of representing anything a merchant intends to bring to market in the future: a phone, SaaS service, book, hotel, restaurant, vehicle, event, development, fashion collection, etc.

So internally:

> **Future Offering (FO)**

can remain unchanged.

The mistake would be requiring consumers and merchants to speak our architecture's language.

### 2. “Future Offering Platform” sounds engineered

We agreed that:

> **Future Offering Platform that delivers Market Anticipation Intelligence as a Service**

is accurate, but sounds constructed and somewhat AI-coined.

The problem isn't necessarily either concept individually. It's asking those coined terms to explain Sagrenti publicly.

Our public language should sound like something people naturally say.

### 3. “Market Anticipation Intelligence” doesn't need to carry the brand

We explored:

**Market Foresight · Signals · Market Pulse · Early Signals · Early Look · First Look · Pre-Market · Pre-Market Signals · Pre-Launch Signals**

The important discovery was that we may not need to invent a replacement category at all.

Ordinary language does the job:

> **People show interest long before they buy. Sagrenti helps merchants see it first.**

And:

> **Sagrenti turns early interest — Watch, Waitlist, Early Access, Beta, Preorder — into a clear picture of what people want before launch.**

Internally/commercially, **Anticipation Intelligence** can still have a precise meaning where we need one.

### 4. “Future Offers” was better—but still problematic

**Future Offers** sounded substantially more natural than *Future Offerings*.

But *offer* has an established commerce meaning associated with **discounts, promotions and deals**.

Given Sagrenti's move away from its earlier Deals identity, that ambiguity isn't especially desirable.

### 5. “Upcoming Releases” uncovered the right direction

**Upcoming Releases** sounded immediately human:

> Upcoming phone releases
> Upcoming game releases
> Upcoming software releases

But it doesn't naturally encompass everything Sagrenti supports.

People don't normally call a new hotel or restaurant a *release*. They call it an **opening**.

That led us to:

> **Upcoming releases, openings and events**

which works well when those distinctions are useful.

### 6. Then we found the umbrella: “Coming Up”

Rather than inventing a noun capable of encompassing releases, openings, launches, events, developments, services, etc., ordinary English already provides the concept:

> **Coming Up**

It works without requiring everything on Sagrenti to belong to the same linguistic category.

And most importantly:

> **What's coming up?**

is something people actually say.

### 7. This gives the two sides of Sagrenti very natural propositions

For consumers:

> **See what's coming up.**

For merchants:

> **See who's interested before you launch.**

And we discovered a particularly punchy merchant expression:

> **Coming up? Put it on Sagrenti.**

### 8. “Coming Up” may create desirable merchant behavior

This was perhaps the most important strategic observation.

Being part of **what's coming up** inherently encourages merchants to publish **early**.

The value chain becomes:

**Merchant publishes early → consumers discover what's coming → early interest accumulates → Sagrenti turns that activity into signals → merchant understands interest before launch.**

A merchant that waits until launch has already surrendered much of the value.

That potentially creates a cultural expectation:

> *If you're bringing something important to market, get it onto Sagrenti early.*

And eventually even competitive pressure: merchants see competing products, openings, events, etc. already accumulating interest while theirs isn't represented.

### 9. This gives us a clean three-layer vocabulary

I think this is where we ultimately landed:

**Engineering/domain**

> **Future Offering**

Precise, universal internal abstraction.

**Consumer**

> **What's Coming Up?**
> **See what's coming up.**

Human discovery language.

**Merchant**

> **People show interest long before they buy. Sagrenti helps merchants see it first.**
>
> **Coming up? Put it on Sagrenti.**

And beneath all of that, Sagrenti is still doing exactly what we designed it to do: turning Watch, Waitlist, Early Access, Beta, Reservation Interest and Preorder Intent into meaningful evidence of consumer anticipation.

We didn't really rename the architecture.

**We stopped making customers learn the architecture in order to understand Sagrenti.**


### 10. We converged on the public-facing term **Signals**, specifically the idea of **early signals**.

The mapping was becoming:

**Internal/domain:** Anticipation Intelligence
**Public-facing:** **Signals / Early Signals**

So instead of telling a merchant:

> Sagrenti delivers Market Anticipation Intelligence.

we can say:

> **Sagrenti turns early interest into signals merchants can act on before launch.**

Or even more simply:

> **See the signals before you launch.**

The particularly nice thing is that **Signals** works naturally with **Coming Up**:

> **See what's coming up. See the signals forming around it.**

So I would correct the final vocabulary map to:

| Internal / architectural  | Public language                                                            |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Future Offering           | **Coming Up / What's Coming Up**                                           |
| Anticipation Intelligence | **Signals / Early Signals**                                                |
| Engagement activity       | Watch, Waitlist, Early Access, Beta, Reservation Interest, Preorder Intent |

I think **Early Signals** is probably the fuller public name, while **Signals** becomes the everyday UI vocabulary: *Signals, Signal activity, Strong signals, Emerging signals*, etc.


**What's coming up? See the signals before you launch.**

It joins the two sides of Sagrenti in one thought:

**“What's coming up?”** → what consumers discover and engage with.
**“See the signals before you launch.”** → what merchants gain from that activity.

And underneath:

**Future Offering** → **Coming Up**
**Anticipation Intelligence** → **Signals**

It's short, human, memorable, and—most importantly—it describes what Sagrenti does without first requiring someone to understand Sagrenti terminology.
