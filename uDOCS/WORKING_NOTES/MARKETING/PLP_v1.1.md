# Sagrenti — Public Language & Positioning

## PLP v1.01 — Consolidated Summary

### 1. Public Language and Domain Language

Sagrenti maintains a deliberate separation between stable internal domain terminology and human-facing public language.

**Future Offering (FO)** is the canonical internal/domain term for a genuine proposition that a merchant intends or is considering bringing to market in the future. It is broad enough to encompass products, software, services, books, vehicles, hotels, restaurants, events, developments, experiences, fashion collections, and other forthcoming propositions.

**Anticipation Intelligence** is the canonical internal/commercial concept for the intelligence Sagrenti derives from measurable consumer anticipation around a Future Offering.

Consumers and merchants should not need to learn this architecture in order to understand Sagrenti.

The governing positioning principle is:

> **Keep the precision of the architecture internally. Use ordinary human language publicly.**

### 2. Coming Up

The intended public-facing expression of **Future Offering** is:

> **Coming Up**

Coming Up provides ordinary language broad enough to encompass releases, openings, launches, events, services, developments, and other forthcoming propositions without forcing them under an artificial common noun.

The core consumer proposition is:

> **See what's coming up.**

Coming Up is more than a public label for individual Future Offerings. It names the market-facing stage Sagrenti introduces before major merchant commitment.

### 3. Signals

The public-facing expression of **Anticipation Intelligence** is:

> **Signals**

Where greater context is useful, **Early Signals** may also be used.

Signals arise from voluntary consumer engagement with what's coming up, including:

**Watch · Waitlist · Early Access · Beta · Reservation Interest · Preorder Intent**

The relationship is straightforward:

> **Consumers engage with what's coming up. That engagement produces Signals merchants can learn from.**

Accordingly:

| Internal / Architectural  | Public Language                                                                |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Future Offering           | **Coming Up / What's Coming Up**                                               |
| Anticipation Intelligence | **Signals / Early Signals**                                                    |
| Engagement activity       | **Watch, Waitlist, Early Access, Beta, Reservation Interest, Preorder Intent** |

### 4. The Missing Stage of Commerce

The conventional conceptual sequence for bringing something to market is:

> **Idea → Build → Launch → Sell**

Merchants have long used market research, focus groups, prototypes, test markets, waitlists, preorders, betas, crowdfunding, and other mechanisms before launch.

These mechanisms, however, remain fragmented rather than constituting a normal market-facing stage where consumers discover what's coming and merchants observe measurable Signals while important decisions can still be changed.

Sagrenti introduces that stage:

> **Idea → Coming Up → Build → Launch → Sell**

**Coming Up is the market-facing stage between Idea and Build where genuine merchant intent can encounter genuine consumer interest before major commitment.**

Sagrenti's long-term ambition is:

> **Coming Up should become as natural a stage of commerce as Launch.**

### 5. Coming Up Exists Before Commitment

A merchant entering Coming Up may not yet have decided whether, what, where, or when to build and launch.

That uncertainty is not a deficiency. It is part of the purpose of the stage.

The central merchant proposition is:

> **See the signals before you commit.**

Coming Up allows market evidence to become visible while that evidence can still influence the merchant's decisions.

ANTi Activation therefore represents readiness to expose genuine future intent and begin observing Signals. It does **not** itself represent a commitment to build or launch.

### 6. Merchant Decision Support

Coming Up is intended to help merchants make better-informed decisions, not merely measure popularity.

Signals should help merchants answer questions such as:

> **Is there enough interest to build this?**

> **What version should we build?**

> **Where is the interest?**

> **When might we launch?**

The core decision flow is:

> **Publish → Consumer Engagement → Signals → Merchant Decision**

Watch, Waitlist, Early Access, Beta, Reservation Interest, Preorder Intent, and other supported engagements are therefore not ends in themselves. They provide observable evidence from which merchants can better understand what the market is telling them.

### 7. Launch Timing

A launch date is not required for ANTi Activation.

The merchant may provide an anticipated launch date or window when one exists, but the absence of one does not prevent participation in Coming Up.

Launch timing may itself be informed by the Signals gathered during the Coming Up stage.

An anticipated launch date or window may therefore be established later or evolve as the merchant learns.

> **ANTi Activation does not imply a commitment to launch.**

### 8. Coming Up Does Not Guarantee Launch

Presence in Coming Up represents genuine merchant intent, not a promise that the proposition will ultimately reach the market.

A proposition may evolve, be postponed, or never proceed to Build or Launch.

This is consistent with the purpose of Coming Up. If Signals influence a merchant not to proceed, or to materially change what was originally contemplated, the pre-commitment stage has performed its function.

Public language and product presentation must therefore avoid implying that presence in Coming Up guarantees eventual production, release, opening, availability, or launch.

### 9. Early Publication

The value of Coming Up increases when merchants publish while meaningful decisions remain open.

The operating dynamic is:

> **Publish early → Be discovered → Accumulate engagement → Observe Signals → Make better-informed decisions**

Publishing only when launch is imminent sacrifices much of the opportunity to learn from consumer response before significant commitments become difficult or expensive to change.

Coming Up therefore creates a natural incentive for merchants to bring genuine future intent into the market earlier.

The resulting merchant publishing proposition is:

> **Coming up? Put it on Sagrenti.**

### 10. Consumer and Merchant Positioning

Sagrenti serves two complementary audiences.

For consumers:

> **See what's coming up.**

For merchants:

> **Let Coming Up show you who's interested before you launch.**

The broader merchant value proposition is:

> **See the signals before you commit.**

And the underlying observation connecting both sides is:

> **People show interest long before they buy. Sagrenti helps merchants see it first.**

A compact expression of the two-sided proposition is:

> **What's coming up? See the signals before you launch.**

### 11. What's Next — Working Decoy

Within **FOPSC v1.0**, **What's Next** remains the working decoy for the intended consumer-facing term **Coming Up**.

The vocabulary distinction is:

| Purpose                            | Term                |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| Canonical engineering/domain term  | **Future Offering** |
| Working public decoy in FOPSC v1.0 | **What's Next**     |
| Intended/protected public language | **Coming Up**       |

**What's Next should not be mechanically replaced with Coming Up in FOPSC v1.0.**

Engineering should continue to implement stable domain semantics and runtime-configurable public terminology rather than unnecessarily embedding public vocabulary into the architecture.

### 12. Positioning and Architecture

PLP establishes the product and market proposition.

Its central doctrine is:

> **See the signals before you commit.**

That doctrine has architectural and commercial consequences, but those consequences belong in their respective canonical documents.

In particular, the conclusion that ANTi service relationships are open-ended and require no predetermined duration belongs canonically in **FOCA**, while the calendar mechanics necessary to support continuing monthly periods belong in **STCD**.

PLP establishes the product reality those architectures must support:

> **A merchant can enter Coming Up before knowing whether, what, where, or when to build and launch.**

### 13. Consolidated Positioning Doctrine

Sagrenti introduces **Coming Up** as a normal market-facing stage before major merchant commitment:

> **Idea → Coming Up → Build → Launch → Sell**

During Coming Up, consumers discover genuine forthcoming propositions and voluntarily engage with those that interest them.

That engagement creates **Signals**.

Those Signals allow merchants to understand what the market is saying while consequential decisions can still be influenced.

For consumers:

> **See what's coming up.**

For merchants:

> **See the signals before you commit.**

Sagrenti's ambition is not merely to provide another analytics or market-research mechanism.

> **Sagrenti seeks to make Coming Up a normal stage of commerce.**

**We keep the architecture precise and make the proposition human.**
