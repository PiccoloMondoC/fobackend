# 1. Executive Summary

Sagrenti’s v1 execution objective is not to replace affiliate commerce.

Sagrenti’s objective is to make merchant-directed commerce more valuable by powering it with measurable anticipation.

The corrected execution thesis is:

> **Sagrenti is anticipation-powered, merchant-directed referral commerce infrastructure.**

Sagrenti helps merchants generate visibility, measurable anticipation, trust-checked launch discovery, and attributable commerce routing.

Sagrenti does not replace the merchant’s store.

Sagrenti does not host checkout.

Sagrenti does not own fulfillment.

Sagrenti does not become the merchant of record.

The v1 execution target is:

> **Front Row + My Radar + Trust Review + Referral Routing + Launch Watch Density + attributable merchant commerce.**

## **2A. Architectural Responsibility Separation** *(new section)*

> ### 2A. Architectural Responsibility Separation
Sagrenti separates **architecture**, **engineering**, and **operations** into distinct responsibilities.

**Architecture** defines platform capabilities and their governing relationships.

**Engineering** implements those capabilities together with the extension points required for safe evolution.

**Administration** governs the operational state of those capabilities through approved configuration rather than software modification.

This separation preserves a stable platform architecture while allowing operational policy to evolve independently of engineering releases.

Consequently:

* Engineering creates capabilities.
* Administration configures capabilities.
* Business operates capabilities.

Supported operational changes should be achieved through governed administrative configuration rather than source-code modification, recompilation, or redeployment.

New capabilities, new extension points, and changes to architectural contracts remain Engineering responsibilities.

> **Sagrenti uses anticipation to improve attributable merchant commerce, not abandon it.**

# 6. Business Model Architecture

## 6.1 Front Row

Front Row is:

> **future commerce infrastructure.**

Merchant pays for:

* launch visibility
* anticipation measurement
* launch presence
* verified merchant presentation
* basic proof reporting

Possible pricing:

* setup fee
* waived setup fee for founding partners
* monthly subscription
* annual subscription

Front Row monetization is not directly dependent on completed sales.

Its primary value is:

> **measurable consumer anticipation before and around launch.**

---

## 6.2 Launch Campaigns

Launch Campaigns are:

> **present-commerce merchandising.**

They are distinct from Front Row.

Launch Campaigns support:

* active promotion
* present-tense merchandising
* launch-period visibility
* deal-style activation
* traffic routing to merchant commerce pages
* affiliate/referral-network compatible attribution where applicable

Initial pricing:

> free or bundled during v1.

Launch Campaigns should not be confused with Front Row subscriptions.

---

## 6.3 Launch Intelligence Fee

The Launch Intelligence Fee is the performance-economics layer.

It may be triggered by:

* preorder activity
* attributable launch traffic
* post-launch referral commerce
* tracked campaign activity
* merchant-agreed conversion events

This is the architectural successor to vague “affiliate commission” language.

The name may evolve, but the concept is stable:

> **Sagrenti earns from attributable merchant commerce produced or informed by launch intelligence.**

The Launch Intelligence Fee may be:

* merchant-direct
* negotiated
* percentage-based
* flat-fee-based
* event-based
* based on merchant-confirmed activity
* based on tracked preorder, launch, referral, or campaign performance

It is not required to depend on a legacy affiliate network.

---

## 6.4 Deals

Deals are not Sagrenti’s identity.

But deals remain:

> **an important monetization and utility layer.**

Deals support:

* immediate consumer utility
* affiliate/referral revenue
* traffic monetization
* price intelligence
* merchant campaign activation
* present-commerce discovery

Deals must not be removed, minimized into irrelevance, or treated as strategically embarrassing.

The corrected hierarchy is:

* **Future:** Front Row + My Radar
* **Present:** Deals + Launch Campaigns
* **Past:** price history + commerce intelligence

# 7. Trust Review & Moderation Architecture

SEA v1.01 replaces “Editorial Review” with:

> **Trust Review & Moderation**

This is not newsroom editorial.

It is operational trust governance.

## 7.1 Default Rule

Default:

> **automation-first trust review.**

Human intervention:

> **exception-based.**

## 7.2 Trust Review Purpose

Trust Review exists to prevent:

* fraudulent launches
* misleading claims
* unsafe merchant representations
* deceptive sponsorship
* fake scarcity
* abusive merchant behavior
* low-quality launch spam
* consumer-harmful routing

## 7.3 Merchant Trust Progression

Merchant trust should progress through:

1. unverified merchant
2. verified merchant
3. trusted merchant
4. fast-track merchant

Fast-track does not mean ungoverned.

It means:

> reduced friction because prior behavior supports trust.

## 7.4 Human Review Triggers

Human review should be triggered by exceptions such as:

* new merchant
* failed verification
* unusual claims
* consumer complaints
* suspicious traffic
* high-risk category
* repeated changes after approval
* automation uncertainty
* elevated commercial exposure
* prior trust violation

## 7.5 Public Promise

Sagrenti should not promise “editorially reviewed launches.”

The safer promise is:

> **trusted launches from verified merchants**

or:

> **trust-checked launch intelligence**

# 14. Referral Routing and Attribution

Sagrenti must route commerce outward.

## 14.1 Routing Rule

All consumer purchase activity must route to the merchant or authorized commerce destination.

Sagrenti may track:

* outbound clicks
* launch source
* campaign source
* Radar source
* timestamp
* category context
* anonymized/referral context
* affiliate/referral identifiers where applicable
* merchant-direct attribution identifiers where affiliate systems do not exist

Sagrenti must not become checkout owner.

## 14.2 Attribution Rule

Attribution should support:

* affiliate network parameters for Deals and Launch Campaigns where applicable
* merchant direct referral parameters
* Launch Campaign parameters
* Front Row launch source parameters
* Radar-origin indicators where appropriate
* merchant-confirmed conversion events
* custom launch economics
* negotiated Launch Intelligence Fee structures

## 14.3 Front Row Attribution Rule

Front Row must be:

> **referral-compatible but not affiliate-dependent.**

Front Row may use affiliate/referral networks where they are appropriate and available.

Front Row must also support merchant-direct attribution where no affiliate infrastructure exists.

This prevents Front Row from being reduced to legacy affiliate routing.

## 14.4 Privacy Rule

Merchant reporting must be aggregate-first.

Do not expose individual consumer identity to merchants unless a separate, explicit, lawful, user-consented workflow exists.
