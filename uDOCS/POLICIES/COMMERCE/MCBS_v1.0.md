# Merchant Commercial & Billing Strategy

## MCBS v1.0

### 1. Strategic Purpose

The Merchant Commercial & Billing Strategy defines how Sagrenti earns revenue from merchants while preserving the integrity of its commerce architecture.

Sagrenti is not merely a discount directory, affiliate surface, campaign board, or outbound traffic broker. Sagrenti is an anticipation intelligence platform expressed through a consumer offer product.

The merchant commercial model must therefore support two simultaneous objectives:

1. Generate early and recurring revenue through practical present-commerce activity.
2. Protect and validate the higher-value future-commerce engine that makes Sagrenti strategically defensible.

The billing model must not obscure this distinction. Merchant fees are not interchangeable. Each fee type represents a different kind of value created by Sagrenti.

Setup fees price onboarding and commercial activation. Subscription fees price continued platform access. Campaign Performance Fees price measurable present-commerce campaign outcomes. Launch Intelligence Fees price measurable future-commerce anticipation signals.

This document is the authoritative commercial and billing reference for merchant plans, fee categories, founding merchant credits, and the CE-recommended credit architecture.

---

### 2. Commercial Philosophy

Sagrenti’s merchant commercial philosophy is based on five principles.

#### 2.1 Charge for Value Created

Sagrenti charges merchants when the platform creates identifiable commercial value.

That value may take different forms:

* access to Sagrenti’s merchant infrastructure;
* placement within Sagrenti’s offer ecosystem;
* qualified consumer engagement;
* measurable campaign outcomes;
* first-party launch anticipation signals;
* merchant intelligence derived from consumer intent.

The commercial model must therefore distinguish platform access from measurable outcomes. Access fees and performance fees serve different functions and should not be collapsed into one undifferentiated charge.

#### 2.2 Preserve the Anticipation Intelligence Signal

Sagrenti’s most strategically important merchant value is not generic traffic. It is measured consumer anticipation before a product is commercially available.

Launch Intelligence Fees are the clearest commercial proof that merchants are willing to pay for anticipation intelligence. They should be protected from broad waivers, promotional dilution, and ambiguous credit treatment.

A merchant who pays for launch intelligence is validating the central Sagrenti thesis: that future consumer intent has measurable commercial value before a market transaction occurs.

#### 2.3 Separate Adoption Incentives from Value Waivers

Founding merchant incentives exist to reduce adoption friction, not to imply that Sagrenti’s services lack value.

For this reason, founding merchant benefits should be modeled as credits rather than silent waivers. A credit preserves the listed economic value of the fee while giving the merchant a defined benefit.

Waiving a fee erases price memory. Applying a credit preserves price architecture.

#### 2.4 Keep Merchant Plans Legible

Merchant plans must be understandable without internal explanation.

A merchant should be able to see the commercial logic:

* Launch Campaigns support available offers and present-commerce conversion.
* Front Row supports future-commerce launch anticipation and intelligence.
* Premium access costs more because it includes higher-value intelligence and strategic launch visibility.
* Activity-based fees apply when Sagrenti produces measurable commercial events.

#### 2.5 Build Billing for Policy Evolution

Commercial policy will evolve. The billing architecture must not require schema redesign every time Sagrenti adjusts pricing, credits, eligibility, campaigns, or founding merchant terms.

The correct architecture is therefore policy-driven:

* fee types are explicit;
* credit eligibility is explicit;
* credits are balances;
* ledger entries preserve billing truth;
* calculations are auditable;
* activity fees remain distinguishable by value category.

### 3. Merchant Plans

Sagrenti merchant plans are organized around the merchant’s commercial objective.

There are two primary merchant plan families:

1. Launch Campaigns
2. Front Row

#### 3.1 Launch Campaigns

Launch Campaigns are present-commerce merchant campaigns for commercially available offers.

They support merchants who want Sagrenti to help drive measurable consumer action for products that can already be purchased, reserved, claimed, or otherwise acted upon commercially.

Launch Campaigns may be used by:

* merchants without affiliate-network infrastructure;
* merchants running direct campaigns;
* merchants promoting available products;
* merchants converting existing demand;
* merchants graduating a previously watched Trend into an available Deal.

Launch Campaigns belong to Present Commerce.

Their primary monetization components are:

* setup fee, where applicable;
* subscription fee;
* Campaign Performance Fee.

Launch Campaigns are compatible with affiliate commerce but are not dependent on affiliate networks.

#### 3.2 Front Row

Front Row is Sagrenti’s merchant-facing future-commerce plan.

It allows merchants to expose upcoming products as Trends and receive first-party anticipation intelligence from Sagrenti-owned consumer behavior.

Front Row supports:

* watch behavior;
* waitlist intent;
* early-access interest;
* preorder interest;
* repeat engagement;
* anticipation analytics;
* merchant launch intelligence.

Front Row belongs to Future Commerce.

Its primary monetization components are:

* setup fee;
* premium subscription fee;
* Launch Intelligence Fee.

Front Row includes Launch Campaigns entitlement because a product that begins as a Trend may later graduate into a Deal. However, Front Row must never be reduced to campaign traffic. Its strategic value is anticipation intelligence before availability.

---

### 4. Setup Fee Philosophy

Setup fees exist to price merchant activation.

A setup fee may cover the commercial value of:

* merchant onboarding;
* account activation;
* plan configuration;
* offer readiness;
* campaign eligibility;
* launch program setup;
* quality review;
* billing readiness;
* merchant trust and compliance checks.

The setup fee is not the core revenue engine. It is a legitimate business fee that prices activation friction and discourages low-intent merchant accounts.

#### 4.1 Launch Campaign Setup Fee

Launch Campaign setup fees may be modest, optional, waived, or promotional depending on acquisition strategy.

Because Launch Campaigns support present-commerce revenue, Sagrenti may choose to reduce setup friction for merchants who can quickly generate campaign volume.

#### 4.2 Front Row Setup Fee

Front Row setup fees should be higher than Launch Campaign setup fees.

Front Row merchants receive access to a more strategically valuable system: Sagrenti’s future-commerce anticipation layer. The setup process for Front Row is also more commercially sensitive because it affects how upcoming products are represented, tracked, measured, and converted into merchant intelligence.

#### 4.3 Founding Merchant Setup Treatment

Founding merchants may receive credits sufficient to cover the setup fee. This should be represented as credit application, not as a disappearance of the setup fee from the billing model.

The invoice may show the setup fee and the credit offset. That preserves price integrity while honoring the founding merchant benefit.

---

### 5. Subscription Philosophy

Subscription fees price ongoing merchant access to Sagrenti’s platform.

Subscriptions should be attached to plan access, not merely usage volume. A merchant pays because Sagrenti provides ongoing commercial infrastructure, visibility, analytics, and platform capabilities.

Subscription fees create recurring revenue, establish plan seriousness, and support predictable platform economics.

#### 5.1 Launch Campaign Subscription

The Launch Campaign subscription should be the entry merchant plan.

It gives merchants access to present-commerce campaign capabilities, campaign analytics, and merchant-direct campaign infrastructure where applicable.

The plan should be priced to reduce friction while still affirming that Sagrenti is a commercial platform, not a free listing service.

#### 5.2 Front Row Premium Subscription

Front Row should be positioned as the premium merchant plan.

It gives merchants access to future-commerce capabilities, Trend placement, anticipation analytics, launch intelligence, and downstream Launch Campaign readiness.

Front Row pricing should reflect the value of early market signal discovery. Merchants are not merely paying for placement; they are paying to understand whether consumers care before full commercial release.

---

### 6. Monthly vs Annual Billing

Sagrenti supports monthly and annual billing.

Monthly billing reduces commitment friction and allows merchants to validate the platform with lower upfront cost.

Annual billing improves revenue predictability and should be rewarded with a modest discount.

#### 6.1 Monthly Billing

Monthly billing should remain available for both Launch Campaigns and Front Row.

It is appropriate for:

* early merchants;
* merchants testing a product launch;
* seasonal merchants;
* merchants with uncertain campaign calendars;
* merchants validating Sagrenti as a channel.

Monthly billing should not be treated as inferior. It is a valid commercial path.

#### 6.2 Annual Billing

Annual billing should be encouraged through a modest discount.

The discount should reward commitment without undermining the monthly price anchor. The annual plan should communicate confidence and long-term partnership.

Annual billing is especially appropriate for Front Row merchants with recurring launch calendars or a pipeline of upcoming products.

### 7. Campaign Performance Fees

Campaign Performance Fees apply to Launch Campaigns and present-commerce outcomes.

They price measurable commercial performance for available offers.

Campaign Performance Fees may be triggered by defined campaign outcomes such as:

* qualified routing;
* verified campaign engagement;
* merchant-confirmed conversion;
* tracked purchase;
* reservation;
* signup;
* claim;
* other campaign-defined present-commerce events.

Campaign Performance Fees belong to Present Commerce.

They should be distinct from affiliate commissions. Affiliate commerce may produce external referral economics, but Launch Campaigns allow Sagrenti to monetize merchant-direct present-commerce activity.

Campaign Performance Fees are not eligible for founding merchant credits.

This is intentional. Campaign Performance Fees are outcome-based charges. If Sagrenti produces measurable commercial performance, the merchant should pay for that performance. Crediting these fees would weaken the relationship between delivered value and billed value.

---

### 8. Launch Intelligence Fees

Launch Intelligence Fees apply to Front Row and future-commerce outcomes.

They price measurable anticipation intelligence generated before a product becomes commercially available as a Deal.

Launch Intelligence Fees may be associated with outcomes such as:

* discovery of a Trend by relevant consumers;
* watch behavior;
* waitlist interest;
* early-access interest;
* preorder interest;
* repeat Trend engagement;
* qualified merchant launch intelligence events;
* other Sagrenti-owned anticipation signals.

Launch Intelligence Fees belong to Future Commerce.

They are not traffic fees. They are not affiliate commissions. They are not ordinary campaign performance fees. They represent payment for first-party consumer anticipation data generated within Sagrenti.

#### 8.1 Strategic Importance

Launch Intelligence Fees are the cleanest validation signal for Sagrenti’s core thesis.

A merchant paying a Launch Intelligence Fee is not merely paying for clicks. The merchant is paying because Sagrenti has identified and measured consumer anticipation before the product is broadly available.

That is the heart of Sagrenti’s defensibility.

For this reason, Launch Intelligence Fees should not be covered by founding merchant credits. Preserving these fees ensures that Sagrenti can test whether merchants will pay for anticipation intelligence as a distinct value category.

If Sagrenti waives Launch Intelligence Fees during the very period when it needs to validate merchant willingness to pay, it weakens the evidence needed to prove the business model.

#### 8.2 Engineering Implication

Billing systems must keep Launch Intelligence Fees structurally distinct from setup fees, subscription fees, and Campaign Performance Fees.

This distinction must be visible in fee classification, billable event records, ledger entries, analytics, merchant invoices, and revenue reporting.

The system must be able to answer:

* how much revenue came from access;
* how much came from present-commerce performance;
* how much came from future-commerce intelligence;
* which merchants paid for anticipation intelligence;
* which anticipation outcomes produced billable value.

### 9. Founding Merchant Programme

The Founding Merchant Programme exists to acquire early merchant partners while preserving Sagrenti’s commercial architecture.

Founding merchants may receive preferential terms in exchange for early participation, feedback, trust, and market proof.

The programme should communicate status and partnership, not discount dependency.

Recommended founding merchant benefits include:

* waived or credit-covered activation economics;
* founding merchant credit balance;
* premium access period;
* Founding Launch Partner recognition;
* priority onboarding;
* early access to Front Row capabilities;
* merchant feedback channel.

The founding programme should be limited, named, and controlled. It should not become the default merchant pricing model.

---

### 10. Founding Merchant Credits

Founding merchant incentives should be modeled as credits.

A founding credit is a monetary balance attached to a merchant billing account. It may be applied only to fee types marked as credit eligible.

This preserves commercial clarity.

The merchant sees that Sagrenti charges real fees. The credit shows that Sagrenti is granting a defined founding benefit.

#### 10.1 Recommended Credit Amount

The founding merchant credit should approximately equal:

* Setup Fee
* six months Premium subscription

This provides a meaningful incentive without removing the value signal from performance and intelligence fees.

The amount may be represented as a currency-denominated balance rather than as a hard-coded number of months. This gives Sagrenti pricing flexibility while preserving the business intent.

#### 10.2 Credit Eligible Fees

Founding credits should be eligible for:

* Setup Fee
* Subscription Fee

These are access and activation charges. Reducing them lowers adoption friction without corrupting outcome validation.

#### 10.3 Credit Ineligible Fees

Founding credits should not be eligible for:

* Launch Intelligence Fee
* Campaign Performance Fee

These are outcome-based fees. They represent measurable value created by Sagrenti.

Applying credits to these fees would blur whether merchants are truly willing to pay when Sagrenti produces commercial outcomes.

---

### 11. CE Recommendation on Credit Design

The CE recommendation is authoritative:

Founding merchant credits should be modeled as a credit balance rather than waived fees.

Fee types should explicitly indicate whether they are credit eligible.

The founding credit should approximately equal:

* Setup Fee
* six months Premium subscription

Credit eligible:

* Setup Fee
* Subscription Fee

Credit ineligible:

* Launch Intelligence Fee
* Campaign Performance Fee

#### 11.1 Reasoning

This design preserves pricing integrity, supports policy evolution, and protects the most important validation signals.

A waiver is commercially blunt. It hides the fee and weakens future pricing discipline.

A credit balance is commercially precise. It allows Sagrenti to show the fee, apply the founding benefit, and preserve the real value of the plan.

Explicit credit eligibility prevents confusion. Sagrenti can change credit amounts, plan prices, and promotional policies without changing the meaning of fee types.

Most importantly, this design protects Launch Intelligence Fees.

Sagrenti must know whether merchants will pay for anticipation intelligence. Launch Intelligence Fees are the clearest evidence of that willingness. They should remain payable even for founding merchants because they validate the central commercial thesis of Sagrenti.

Campaign Performance Fees should also remain payable because they represent present-commerce performance. When Sagrenti produces measurable campaign outcomes, those outcomes should retain commercial value.

### 12. Billing Architecture Principles

Sagrenti’s billing architecture must support the following principles.

#### 12.1 Explicit Fee Types

Every billable merchant event must belong to an explicit fee type.

At minimum:

* setup fee;
* subscription fee;
* Campaign Performance Fee;
* Launch Intelligence Fee.

Each fee type must carry commercial meaning.

#### 12.2 Credit Eligibility Flag

Each fee type must explicitly indicate whether credits may apply.

Credit eligibility is a property of fee policy, not an assumption buried in billing logic.

#### 12.3 Ledger Truth

Credits, charges, adjustments, payments, and settlements must be represented through auditable ledger entries.

The ledger must preserve what happened, not merely the current balance.

#### 12.4 Outcome Distinction

Billing must distinguish access fees from outcome fees.

This distinction is essential for revenue analysis, merchant reporting, and strategic validation.

#### 12.5 Merchant-Level Balance

Merchant billing accounts may maintain balances, deposits, credits, and payable amounts.

The architecture must support merchant-level financial state while preserving event-level billing detail.

---

### 13. Revenue Interpretation

Sagrenti revenue must be interpreted by value category.

#### 13.1 Access Revenue

Access revenue includes setup and subscription fees.

It indicates merchant willingness to access the platform.

#### 13.2 Present-Commerce Outcome Revenue

Present-commerce outcome revenue includes Campaign Performance Fees and affiliate/referral economics.

It indicates Sagrenti’s ability to drive action on commercially available offers.

#### 13.3 Future-Commerce Intelligence Revenue

Future-commerce intelligence revenue includes Launch Intelligence Fees.

It indicates merchant willingness to pay for anticipation intelligence before commercial availability.

This is the highest strategic signal.

---

### 14. Permanent Commercial Doctrine

Sagrenti’s merchant billing model must preserve the distinction between access, performance, and intelligence.

Founding merchant incentives must reduce adoption friction without erasing value.

Credits are the correct instrument. Waivers are the wrong default.

Launch Intelligence Fees must remain protected because they validate Sagrenti’s central business: merchants paying for measurable consumer anticipation.

Campaign Performance Fees must remain protected because they validate Sagrenti’s present-commerce execution.

The merchant commercial model must therefore support adoption without weakening proof.

That is the governing commercial doctrine of MCBS v1.0.
